# Game concept decision — DRAGLINE

Six concepts were generated from deliberately different angles, then scored
independently by three judges applying incompatible criteria: a market analyst on
discoverability, a Godot engineer who verified every reuse claim against the actual
vendored tree, and a controller/Steam-Deck player advocate. This records the outcome.

## Scoreboard

| Concept | Total /30 | Backed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DRAGLINE** | 24 | all three judges |
| KEYSTONE | 21.5 | market + player (engineer refused) |
| DATUM | 19 | market + engineer (player refused) |
| RILL | 18 | player only |
| ANTINODE | 18 | engineer only |
| TRAPLINE | 14.5 | none |

## The concepts

- **ANTINODE** — You never touch anything: you move crates, shatter glass and lure hunters by tuning a rig of wave emitters until the interference pattern itself does the work.
- **KEYSTONE** — A demolition game about controlled collapse: read the stress running through a procedurally generated megastructure, cut the one block holding it up, and land the whole thing inside the marked zone.
- **DATUM** — A submarine hunting roguelite where the enemy is never drawn on screen — you find it, track it and kill it entirely from sound, on a gamepad.
- **TRAPLINE** — You are a field biologist with 24 days, a bag of camera traps, and an alien valley whose food web nobody has ever mapped — figure out who eats whom before the mining consortium's land-use hearing, and bet your funding on every claim you file.
- **RILL** — A calm, controller-first sim where you shape a dry valley with nothing but earth and water — and the forest that grows back is one you never planted.
- **DRAGLINE** — You haul freight over a procedurally generated mountain, and every successful delivery bolts another trailer onto your rig — until the physics-jointed tail behind you is longer than the road ahead.

## Recommendation: DRAGLINE

Build DRAGLINE: an arcade physics-hauling roguelite where every successful delivery bolts another physics-jointed trailer onto your rig, until the articulated tail behind you is longer than the road ahead. You drive a generated mountain route depot-to-depot on analog triggers, fight a tail whose momentum arrives half a second late, winch dropped cargo back up scree slopes, and at each depot choose whether to couple another trailer (more score, worse handling, new failure modes) or cash one in for an upgrade. Score is cargo delivered multiplied by final tail length, so the greedy line and the safe line pull against each other the whole way down. It wins because it is the only concept in the set whose core toy already exists, tuned and working, inside the vendored library — the driving, the cab-to-trailer 6DOF coupling, the five-link PinJoint winch chain, the chase camera, the speedometer, the gamepad-focus menus, and crucially the Jolt physics configuration are all sitting in third_party/godot-demo-projects/3d/truck_town/ under MIT. That matters more than it sounds: this team's single structural handicap is that it cannot evaluate game feel in a GPU-less container, and DRAGLINE is the only concept that starts from feel someone else already got right rather than gambling three months on discovering it. Everything left to build — chunked heightfield terrain with a road spline, a chain manager, a winch, a run layer — is ordinary engineering with no research in it, and its one genuinely dangerous unknown resolves to a CSV file on day three.

## Why this one

DRAGLINE is both the highest scorer (24/30) and the only concept in the field that no judge voted against. That combination is rare enough to be the argument. KEYSTONE (21.5) was rejected outright by the engineer; DATUM (19) was rejected by the player advocate; RILL, ANTINODE and TRAPLINE each drew two rejections. DRAGLINE's *lowest* judge score is 7.5. More importantly, the unanimity is structural rather than coincidental. Three judges applying three incompatible criteria — buildability, hook legibility, and sofa feel — each independently landed on it or one place off it, and they did so for the same underlying reason: the risky part is already written. The engineer ranked it first because an engineer can have a truck with a four-trailer tail driving over generated ground inside a week. The player advocate ranked it first because minute one already exists in the repository and works on a pad. The market analyst ranked it second because its still frame and its clip are both immediately legible with zero explanation. WHERE THE JUDGES DISAGREED, AND HOW I RESOLVE IT. Disagreement one: the market analyst put KEYSTONE first, DRAGLINE second. The split is about images — KEYSTONE's load-tinted cathedral is a screenshot nobody else on Steam has, while DRAGLINE is built from Kenney CC0 models whose shared colormap.png palette reads as "asset flip" to exactly the cynical buyers who decide whether to click. I do not resolve this by averaging, because the two judges are not disagreeing about the same object. The market judge's case for KEYSTONE rests entirely on the stress heatmap. The engineer's case against KEYSTONE is that the stress heatmap is the single least likely thing in the design to work: PhysicsDirectBodyState2D contact impulses are not structural load, they are noisy, they may confidently light the wrong block, the fix is an unbudgeted FEM or truss solve that cannot be dropped because it is the visual identity — and unlike DRAGLINE there is no Jolt in 2D, so the underlying solver is the one whose tall-stack jitter and sinking a demolition game cannot hide. That is a fatal correlation: KEYSTONE's marketing hook and its highest engineering risk are the same object, and if the risk fires you lose both the game and the store page simultaneously. DRAGLINE has the same correlation structure — its hook is the long tail and its risk is chain stability — but the cost of being wrong is wildly different. Chain stability has known cheap mitigations (keep adjacent mass ratios inside ~5:1, raise the tick to 120 Hz, tuned 6DOF angular limits over naive pins, a velocity watchdog), it runs under a solver specifically good at chains, and it produces a pass/fail answer from a headless soak on day three. KEYSTONE's stress-signal risk cannot be answered until the stress model exists, and its answer might be "write an FEM solver." So I side with the engineer on which concept to build, and with the market analyst on what will kill DRAGLINE if it fails. The asset-flip criticism is correct and I am acting on it rather than dismissing it — see the week-one art kill test below. The contour-band terrain shader is not a nice detail, it is the entire thing standing between this project and being filed as free-assets-on-noise, and it must be validated in week one when it is still free to have a second idea. Disagreement two: the engineer put the VehicleBody3D risk at better than even odds of a three-week detour; the pitch called it second-order. I verified the engineer is quoting the class reference accurately (VehicleBody3D.xml line 10: "This class has known issues and isn't designed to provide realistic 3D vehicle physics"). I side with the engineer on severity but not on the remedy, and there is an engineering move neither the pitch nor any judge made: only the CAB needs to be a driven vehicle. Trailers are not powered — they need suspension, rolling resistance and grip, not engine force or steering. Building trailers as plain RigidBody3D with simple raycast wheels instead of VehicleBody3D cuts 60 wheel raycasts down to 4 driven plus 56 cheap ones, removes 14 instances of the flagged class from the load path, and makes the mass and inertia scaling that keeps the chain stable directly controllable. That should be tested in week one alongside the vendored configuration, and it materially shrinks the risk the engineer is worried about. Disagreement three: the player advocate's ranking is the one I weight most heavily on retention, and its verdict is blunt — DRAGLINE might be a twenty-minute joke, and it obviously wants two-player couch co-op that three months cannot afford. I found something that partly dissolves the second half. third_party/godot-demo-projects/viewport/split_screen_input/ contains InputRoutingViewportContainer, which already filters InputEventJoypadButton by device id. DRAGLINE's co-op does not need split screen at all — one camera, one truck, driver on pad one and winch operator on pad two — so the whole feature reduces to routing actions by device index against code that already exists. That is days, not weeks, and per the player advocate it roughly doubles both stream value and hours played. It is the single highest-leverage thing in this document and it should be an explicit owner decision, not an assumed cut. FINALLY, ONE CORRECTION THAT APPLIES REGARDLESS. Three of the six concepts led with "the underserved intersection is controller-native X." The market judge is right that nobody has ever searched Steam for that. Deck-native is a conversion closer and a curation channel, not a reason anyone discovers you. DRAGLINE is the concept least dependent on that argument, which is another quiet point in its favour — its hook survives without it.

## The strongest case against it

The strongest case against DRAGLINE is that it is a joke with a half-life, wearing clothes borrowed from a free asset pack, and both of those problems compound at exactly the same moment. The hook — a fourteen-segment articulated centipede folded across three hairpins — is funny, and physics-fail comedy is the most reliably reposted content on the internet, which is real free distribution nobody else in this field gets. But it is funny *once*. The audience that shares physics-toy clips is documented to convert poorly into buyers, because the clip is the entire product; watching it is the complete experience. The pitch's answer is that each new trailer unlocks a qualitatively new failure mode rather than a bigger number, but that is an assertion with no evidence behind it, and it is precisely the claim playtesting is most likely to disprove. If trailer nine feels like trailer eight, then the score formula is decoration, the depot choice is fake, and the game is a driving demo that peaked in its own trailer. Layered onto that: the visual identity is Kenney CC0 models sharing one colormap.png palette atlas, plus truck_town's meshes. That palette look is recognised on sight by exactly the cynical Steam users and curators who decide whether an unknown game deserves a click. One contour-band terrain shader is the only thing separating DRAGLINE from being filed as an asset flip, and it is one idea, from a team that has explicitly declared it has no visual designer. Art of Rally is the standing counterexample the pitch leans on, and Art of Rally's whole advantage was a developer with genuine visual taste — the exact resource being declared absent here. There is no fallback plan for the art that does not involve hiring. Now combine them. The plausible bad outcome is not a game that fails loudly. It is a game that gets three million views on a clip somebody else posted, converts a few thousand of them at ten dollars, reviews as "funny for an hour, looks like a Unity tutorial," and leaves the team having spent three months building someone else's viral moment. And the two failure modes reinforce each other: a game whose only defence against the asset-flip read is "but it's funny" needs the joke to keep working long past the point where jokes normally stop. Two smaller but real objections deserve to stay on the record. The engineer put better-than-even odds on VehicleBody3D forcing a three-week custom-suspension detour once masses are scaled and load is applied — that detour would consume all the schedule slack, and the class reference itself warns you off the class. And the terrain generator is the silent failure mode: every hour of content comes from it, the first ten generated legs always look fine, and a generator that produces plausible-but-characterless routes yields a game that reviews as repetitive at month three, by which point it is too late to fix cheaply.

## Kill criteria

Each of these is cheap, dated, and produces a pass/fail answer. Running them is the
plan; skipping them is how three months disappears.

- WEEK ZERO, ONE AFTERNOON, BEFORE ANY CODE — Steam and itch tag research on Driving x Physics x Roguelite, searching specifically for trailer-chain, tow-physics and articulated-vehicle games. Both the pitch and the market judge asked for this and it is the cheapest research that exists. If three or more such games exist and all have under twenty reviews, the niche has been tried; that is not automatically fatal but it forces a price and positioning rethink before a line is written. If one exists and did well, that is validation. Not knowing is the only unacceptable state.
- DAY 3 — HEADLESS TWELVE-TRAILER SOAK, and run it with LOADED cargo masses, not the empty vendored configuration, at both N=12 and N=16. Scripted throttle and steering trace over generated terrain, ten minutes of simulated time, logging max joint separation, peak relative velocity between coupled bodies, rollover count and NaN checks to CSV. PASS: joint separation stays bounded, no body diverges. FAIL after one week of tuning mass ratios, tick rate and joint limits: the escalation fantasy is dead. Rescope to a six-trailer game with harder terrain and a different score formula, or cut. Do not patch and continue.
- WEEK 1 — VEHICLEBODY3D UNDER LOAD. Drive the cab towing eight or more trailers and judge handling on real hardware. Test the cheaper architecture in parallel: trailers as plain RigidBody3D with simple raycast wheels rather than VehicleBody3D, since trailers are never driven. If neither the vendored class nor that variant handles acceptably under load and the fix is not a tuning pass, decide IMMEDIATELY between a three-week custom raycast suspension (which eats every hour of slack in the schedule) or capping max tail length. Deferring this decision past week one is how the three months disappears.
- WEEK 1 — THE ART KILL TEST, one hour, borrowed from TRAPLINE's method. Render a single frame: contour-banded terrain, volumetric fog, headlights raking a slope, a twelve-trailer tail on a switchback. Show it cold to five people who were not told what it is and ask one question — does this look like a game someone made, or a game someone assembled? If it reads as assembled, the contour shader is not carrying the identity and you need a second visual idea BEFORE any content work begins. This is the market judge's objection made testable, and it costs an hour.
- WEEK 2 — THE DESIRE TEST, on a Steam Deck, five people, one instruction: get to the other depot. Then offer them a fifth trailer. Does anyone take it on purpose? Does anyone reverse a long tail back uphill to recover a dropped crate? Does anyone laugh at the winch? If nobody voluntarily makes their own life harder to lengthen the tail, the progression has no engine, the score formula is decoration, and the game is a driving demo with a gimmick. Kill it at two weeks with almost all three months still on the table.
- WEEK 6 — THE GENERATOR TEST, the silent killer with the latest deadline. Generate twenty legs, have someone who did not build the generator drive ten of them blind, and ask them to describe what made each one different. If they cannot distinguish leg three from leg eight, the terrain generator is producing plausible-but-characterless routes and every hour of content downstream will read as repetitive. Fix the generator before building the meta layer, upgrades or unlocks — those are decoration on top of a content engine that does not work.
- ONGOING, NON-NEGOTIABLE — the owner plays a Deck build with a controller every single week starting week one. Feel is the one thing the container cannot judge (docs/CODESPACES.md is explicit that llvmpipe frame times are meaningless), and a green headless test suite is not evidence the truck is pleasant to drive. If two consecutive weeks pass without a Deck play session, the project has lost its only instrument for its most important quality.

## First two weeks

1. DAY 1 — Stand up the project on Godot 4.7.1 with 3d/physics_engine set to Jolt from the first commit (copy the line from third_party/godot-demo-projects/3d/truck_town/project.godot:191 — do not discover this later). Copy in truck_town's vehicles/ wholesale: vehicle.gd (124 lines), follow_camera.gd (95), speedometer.gd (30), meshes.glb and the five .res mesh resources. Get the vendored trailer_truck.tscn driving on flat ground with a controller before touching anything else. This should be a same-day result.
2. DAYS 2-3 — Build the headless soak harness FIRST, before terrain, before the winch, before anything fun. A scripted throttle and steering trace, a runtime-assembled tail of N trailers, ten minutes of simulated time, CSV output of max joint separation, peak inter-body relative velocity, rollover count and NaN checks. Borrow the harness shape from third_party/godot-demo-projects/2d/physics_tests/tests/performance/ and the joint-tuning sandbox from 3d/physics_tests/tests/functional/test_joints.gd. Run it at N=12 and N=16 with loaded cargo masses. This is the project's primary quality instrument and it costs two days.
3. DAYS 3-4 — In parallel with the soak, prototype trailers as plain RigidBody3D with raycast wheels alongside the vendored VehicleBody3D version, and compare stability and cost in the same harness. Only the cab needs to be a driven vehicle; this removes fourteen instances of a class the engine documentation explicitly warns about from the load path and cuts wheel-raycast count dramatically. Whichever wins, that decision is locked for the rest of the project.
4. DAYS 4-7 — Chunked procedural terrain. Seeded FastNoiseLite heightfield meshed on worker threads into ArrayMesh chunks with HeightMapShape3D colliders, streamed around the truck. Take the streaming and chunk-dictionary architecture from third_party/godot-demo-projects/3d/voxel/world/chunk.gd and voxel_world.gd — swap voxel meshing for heightfield meshing, keep everything else. Use third_party/godot-demo-projects/misc/noise_viewer to dial the noise parameters interactively rather than by guessing.
5. DAYS 6-8 — The route generator and the road. A spline from depot A to depot B with controlled grade and curvature and a required count of genuine problems (a washout, a steep grade, a ford, a hairpin too tight for the current tail), terrain vertices flattened to meet it, and Kenney road pieces (road-straight.glb, road-corner.glb, road-split.glb, road-straight-lightposts.glb from third_party/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/models/) instanced along it via MultiMeshInstance3D. Reuse the runtime-MeshLibrary construction half of Starter-Kit-City-Builder/scripts/builder.gd; discard its mouse raycast cursor entirely. Getting routes that are interesting rather than mush is the real work here, not the meshing.
6. DAY 8 — THE ART FRAME, one hour, non-negotiable, before any more content. Write the contour-band terrain shader (fract of world Y over a band interval for the contour lines, slope-based blend between grass, rock and snow, a wetness darkening term), add the volumetric fog recipe from 3d/volumetric_fog, the gradient sky from 3d/sky_shaders, and the per-biome LUT from 3d/tonemap_color_correction. Render one frame with a twelve-trailer tail on a switchback and run the cold-viewer test on five people. If it reads as assembled rather than made, stop and find a second visual idea now.
7. DAYS 9-11 — The winch and the cargo. Spawn the five-link PinJoint3D capsule chain from tow_truck.tscn at runtime between hook and target; nearest-valid-target acquisition with a snap and an on-screen lock indicator, never a cursor; reel-in as a motorised joint with a tension cap; and the anchor state machine that decides whether the winch pulls the target toward the truck or the truck toward the target — which is where the comedy lives and where the money clip comes from. Crates as RigidBody3D that detach above an impact threshold. Use 3d/waypoints/waypoint.gd verbatim for the dropped-crate and depot markers, including the behind-camera detection and screen-edge clamping.
8. DAYS 11-12 — HUD and feel. Drop in speedometer.gd with its gradient tint, add tail count and cargo integrity readouts in the same style, wire the audio pool from third_party/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/scripts/audio.gd (42 lines, pooled AudioStreamPlayer with queue and random pitch) to hitch clanks, crate drops and gravel, and steal the Engine.time_scale plus AudioServer.playback_speed_scale slow-motion pair from 3d/ragdoll_physics for catastrophic tail failure. Add the rear-facing tail camera as a fourth mode in follow_camera.gd. Rumble is already in vehicle.gd via Input.start_joy_vibration — add the off toggle now rather than later.
9. DAY 12 — GRAFT KEYSTONE'S DISTRIBUTION FEATURE WHILE IT IS CHEAP. A 20 Hz transform ring buffer over the truck and tail (thirty seconds is a few megabytes at this body count) plus a free camera, HUD hide and time scale. That gives replay-cam and clip capture — this game's entire marketing thesis is clips, and building the capture path in week two costs a day where retrofitting it in month three costs a week. Pair it with viewport/screen_capture/screen_capture.gd for stills.
10. DAYS 13-14 — Ship a Steam Deck build and run the desire test. Five people, no explanation, one instruction: get to the other depot. Watch what they do when offered a fifth trailer. Do not add menus, upgrades, depot screens, meta-progression or a second biome before this test — everything past this point should be decided by what those five people did. NOT IN SCOPE FOR THESE TWO WEEKS, deliberately: the Maaack shell, Steam integration, object pooling, upgrades, the daily seed, and any second terrain archetype. All of those are real work (Steam and pooling are genuine library gaps — a grep across all 1408 vendored scripts returns zero pooling code) and all of them are worthless if the desire test fails.

## Questions only the owner can answer

- Do you actually want to spend three months on a joke? DRAGLINE's hook is comedy, and comedy needs you to still believe in it on day sixty when it has stopped being funny to you specifically. If the honest answer is that you would rather build something you find beautiful or interesting than something you find funny, say so now — RILL and ANTINODE both scored 18 and both are things a person could love for three months. This is the one question no analysis can answer for you.
- Is two-player same-screen co-op in scope? Per-device joypad routing is already vendored in viewport/split_screen_input/, and because both players share one camera there is no split screen to build. The player advocate believes it roughly doubles both stream value and hours played, and every single viewer will ask for it. It is days rather than weeks, but it touches the input layer everywhere and must be decided before that layer is written, not after.
- Do you have a Steam Deck in your hands on day one? Every kill test in this plan that matters runs on real hardware, docs/CODESPACES.md is explicit that container frame times are meaningless, and this concept was chosen partly because its feel layer is pre-tuned — which is worthless if nobody feels it weekly. If the Deck is two months away, that changes which concept you should build.
- If week one says VehicleBody3D cannot handle a heavy tow, do you accept a three-week custom raycast suspension detour, or is that an automatic scope cut to a six-trailer maximum? This will consume every hour of slack in a three-month schedule. Decide the policy before the test, so the test result does not get argued with.
- What price band are you aiming at? Ten to twelve dollars is the impulse band where a funny clip actually converts into a sale, which is the concept's entire distribution theory. Fifteen or more requires more content depth than three months buys. This determines how much of the meta layer is worth building.
- Is anyone on the team willing to own the visual identity as a real, named job — palette, contour shader, fog and LUT presets, capsule art? The market judge identified this as the number one discoverability liability, there is no artist, and 'the shader will handle it' is currently one idea with no owner and no fallback. If nobody owns it, budget for a contractor for capsule art and one hero screenshot, because those two assets do more selling than everything else combined.
- Has the Steamworks paperwork started? Steam integration and achievements are a genuine library gap — a grep across all 1408 vendored scripts returns zero pooling code and there is no Steam code either — and it is a week of work nobody enjoys plus a day of admin, gating the daily-seed hook. Also be clear-eyed that physics is not deterministic across machines, so the daily seed gives everyone the same terrain but no verifiable runs and no ghost replays. Do not sell it as a leaderboard.
- How do you feel about being told this looks like an asset flip? It will happen, publicly, in a comment thread, probably in month two. If that would derail you, either commit now to the art test in week one and act on its result, or pick a concept whose visual identity is generated rather than assembled — ANTINODE and DATUM both have that property and neither can be accused of using someone else's models.

## Runner-up

KEYSTONE — but conditionally, and not as an automatic fallback. If DRAGLINE dies at the day-three physics test, the correct move is to rescope DRAGLINE (shorter tail, harder terrain) rather than jump to KEYSTONE, because by then you already own a working driving game. KEYSTONE is the runner-up as a genuinely different project, and it should only be started after its own silent-clip kill test passes — the market judge's enthusiasm for it rests on the stress heatmap, which the engineer judge showed is simultaneously the marketing hook and the least likely component to work.

## Ideas worth stealing from the losing concepts

- FROM KEYSTONE — cinema mode as an engineered feature, not a hope. KEYSTONE was the only pitch that treated distribution as something you build: a rewind ring buffer that already exists means free camera, HUD hiding, time scale and clip export are nearly free on top. The market judge called this the single most valuable line in any of the six pitches. DRAGLINE's whole marketing thesis is clips and it currently has no capture path at all. Add a 20 Hz transform ring buffer in week two — it is cheap at this body count and it turns every catastrophic tail failure into a shareable asset the player produces without leaving the game.
- FROM KEYSTONE — make the vendored perf harness the literal first task. KEYSTONE's best process decision was running third_party/godot-demo-projects/2d/physics_tests/tests/performance/ on real hardware on day one to set the body budget, then designing every level under that ceiling rather than discovering it in month three. DRAGLINE needs the 3D equivalent: measure the sustainable awake-body and joint count on a Deck before deciding max tail length, not after.
- FROM RILL — accessibility as art direction rather than an options checkbox. The player advocate asked for this explicitly and named DRAGLINE's accessibility thinking the thinnest of the six. Copy RILL's discipline wholesale: every hold has a toggle equivalent (holding a trigger for a long climb is a hand-cramp tax), all bindings remappable, UI scaled for arm's length at 1280x800 rather than desk distance, plus rumble off, camera-shake and speed-FOV sliders (speed-driven FOV widening layered on heavy volumetric fog is a motion-sickness combination), and an optional cargo-never-falls assist so players can enjoy the toy without the punishment.
- FROM ANTINODE — headless invariant assertions as the honesty mechanism. ANTINODE proposed the best test plan in the set (node spacing equals lambda over two, closed-box modal frequencies, CPU/GPU parity within tolerance) precisely because nobody can look at a Deck most days. DRAGLINE's equivalent: gdUnit4 assertions on joint separation bounds, NaN absence, momentum sanity after coupling and uncoupling, and same-machine determinism of the terrain generator from a seed. Green tests will not tell you the truck is fun, but red tests will stop you shipping a tail that explodes.
- FROM TRAPLINE — the one-hour cold-viewer art test. Build one frame, show it to five people who know nothing, ask what they are looking at. TRAPLINE proposed it for its camera-trap stills; it is exactly the right instrument for DRAGLINE's single largest discoverability liability, and it costs an hour in week one instead of a review in month four.
- FROM DATUM — its schedule warning, applied as budget discipline. DATUM's engineer critique was that 'controller-native diegetic console UI framework' was one bullet that is realistically a third of the calendar, because interface work always expands. DRAGLINE has the same trap in miniature: the depot three-card choice screen, the upgrade flow and the run summary are written as one line each. Reuse truck_town/car_select/car_select.gd's grab_focus chain and Maaack's menu shell rather than designing new UI, and treat any bespoke screen as a week, not an afternoon.
- A LIBRARY FINDING NO JUDGE MADE — third_party/godot-demo-projects/viewport/split_screen_input/ contains InputRoutingViewportContainer, which already filters InputEventJoypadButton by device id, plus root.gd's multi-joypad configuration. DRAGLINE's obvious co-op (one player drives, one works the winch) shares a single camera, so it needs no split screen at all — only per-device action routing, against code that already exists. The player advocate called co-op the thing that would roughly double both stream value and hours played. This is now a days-scale feature rather than a weeks-scale one, and it should be an explicit owner decision rather than an assumed cut.
- AN ENGINEERING STEER NEITHER THE PITCH NOR ANY JUDGE MADE — only the cab needs to be a VehicleBody3D. Trailers are never powered; they need suspension, rolling resistance and grip, not engine force or steering. Building them as plain RigidBody3D with simple raycast wheels removes fourteen instances of a class the 4.7.1 reference explicitly warns against from the load path, cuts wheel-raycast count from sixty to four driven plus fifty-six cheap, and makes the adjacent-body mass ratios that keep the joint chain stable directly controllable. Test both in week one and lock the winner.
